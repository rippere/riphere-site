NovaCRM: the follow-up is already written

An AI-native CRM that turns scattered email and Slack into a prioritized pipeline, and never sends anything without a person's yes.

3 min read

Why I built it

I got tired of keeping my CRM up to date by hand.

Underneath that: I watched capable people drown in operational overhead while their best ideas never shipped. That's the problem I built NovaCRM against. It started as an internal tool for a student organization I founded, then grew into the thing I spent most of my time on for my work.

Who it's for

A founder who sells. The kind of person who still owes a reply from 16 days ago, not because they don't care, but because the inbox, the Slack threads and the deal notes all live in different places and none of them tap you on the shoulder.

What it does

Agents take the scattered email and Slack and turn it into an organized, prioritized pipeline, with a person in the loop on anything that matters.

  • Replies. Open a message and it drafts the reply: subject, body and tone, from the message and the contact.
  • Quiet deals. Every deal carries a health score that drops when it sits too long in a stage or the other side goes silent. Once a day, the deals that have gone quiet get a drafted follow-up, posted to Slack with two buttons: Approve & Send, or Dismiss.
  • The rest of the pipeline. Lead scoring, call transcription and search over contacts by meaning, not just by name.

The follow-up is already written. You just say yes.

How I got here

NovaCRM started as a product. Going through jobs and trying to start a business changed that. Charging per seat for a CRM is just unviable, and the amount of marketing or feature differentiation you'd need to stand out is absurd. More to the point, it's a solved problem: everybody has a CRM, and it's not a difficult thing to build.

So I stopped pursuing it as a product. Now it's part of the package: people who pay for my consulting get access to this tooling, because it is profoundly helpful, and it links into the rest of the work I do for them.

A few things I held onto the whole way:

  • A person approves every draft. The drafted follow-up only sends when someone presses Approve. There is no other path that sends it.
  • The approval lives where people already are. It shows up in Slack, not in yet another inbox you have to remember to check.
  • It doesn't nag. At most 5 drafted follow-ups a day, and never the same deal twice in 7 days.
  • The scoring is explainable. Deal health comes from plain rules (days in stage, days of silence), not a black box, so you can see why a deal got flagged.

I built it with coding agents I direct; the decisions are mine. How I run them is on FL 08.

Results

  • In use by a second department.
  • Live. It runs at app.riphere.com, with the API, background workers and database deployed alongside it.
  • From the code: the drafted follow-up sends only when a person approves it; the quiet-deal check runs once a day, drafts for at most 5 deals, and won't ask about the same deal again for 7 days.

What's next

It keeps running as tooling for the people I consult for. New work on it comes from what those clients need, not from a product roadmap.
